# Campaign Bible: Blades of the Holy War

## 1. DEEP LORE & HISTORY
[TAG: LORE_CORE]

### Origin Myth: The Divine Spark's Fury
In the beginning, the world was a single, perfect **Divine Spark**, holding within it the promise of infinite light and life. But the Divine Spark was fragile, and in a moment of cosmic carelessness, it was struck by a rogue **Cosmic Ray**, causing it to split into countless fragments. From its shattered core, the world was born, a place of constant conflict, dogma, and rebirth. The "Holy" is not just the residue of divine fire; it is the dust of countless shattered beliefs, the memory of every lost dogma. The "War" is not just the rising sun; it is the lingering echo of the Divine Spark's fury, a force that seeks to gather the scattered fragments of the Spark and restore the world to its original, perfect state. The conflict arises because the Divine Spark's fury is so profound that it threatens to consume all of existence in a single, catastrophic act of divine remembrance, burning away all that is, to restore all that was.

### Timeline of Events
1.  **-10,000 Y (The Shattering):** The Divine Spark is struck by a Cosmic Ray, creating the world and its cycle of conflict and dogma.
2.  **-8000 Y (The First Crusade):** The first great civilization rises and falls, its divine dust becoming the foundation of the world.
3.  **-6000 Y (The Dogma Plague):** A powerful echo of the Divine Spark's fury sweeps the world, causing all beings to remember every lost dogma, driving many to madness.
4.  **-4000 Y (The Holy Cult):** Mortals discover the power of faith and form the Holy Cult, seeking to gather the scattered fragments of the Spark.
5.  **-2000 Y (The Great Schism):** A rogue Holy Cultist attempts to force a premature rebirth, resulting in a catastrophic event that causes all beings to forget their past for a century.
6.  **-1000 Y (The Faith-Weavers):** Mortals learn to manipulate the Holy, weaving it into powerful artifacts and wondrous compounds.
7.  **-500 Y (The Echo-Wars):** A powerful echo of the Divine Spark's fury causes all beings to remember every lost dogma, leading to widespread despair and self-destruction.
8.  **-100 Y (The Divine Spark's Whisper):** The Divine Spark, stirring in its slumber, begins to whisper to powerful cultists, tempting them with the promise of ultimate rebirth.
9.  **-10 Y (The Missing Fragment):** A vital fragment of the Divine Spark, the **Essence of Unity**, is stolen from the Holy Cult, causing a wave of despair to spread through the world.
10. **Present Day:** The party are new initiates at the Holy Cult, each with their own unique connection to faith. They are caught in a crisis as the Divine Spark's fury intensifies, and the world threatens to consume all of existence in a single, catastrophic act of divine remembrance.

### The "Why": The Cycle of Conflict
The central conflict is the struggle between **Acceptance and Obsession**. The Divine Spark's fury, though born of loss, has become an obsession, a relentless drive to restore what was, even if it means destroying what is. The world, in its constant cycle of conflict and dogma, represents acceptance, the understanding that loss is a part of life. The Holy Cult, in its pursuit of rebirth, risks becoming consumed by this obsession, burning away all that is, to restore all that was. The party's struggle is not just to become Holy Cultists, but to defend the very concept of acceptance, to prove that life is worth living, even with its imperfections, and to prevent the universe from becoming utterly, irrevocably consumed by fury.

## 2. FACTIONS
[TAG: FACTION_DATA]

### 1. The Holy Cult
**Type:** Scholarly Institution / Faith Mages
**Leader:** The Grand Divine, a legendary figure who claims to be a direct descendant of the Divine Spark.
**Asset:** **The Dogma Archive:** A magical archive that stores the divine dust of every known lost dogma in existence.
**Rivals:** The Nihilists, The Forgetting.

### 2. The Nihilists
**Type:** Anti-Faith Cult / Meaningless Zealots
**Leader:** The Blank Slate, a being whose presence drains all meaning from its surroundings.
**Asset:** **Anti-Faith Auras:** They can create zones where all memories are forgotten, and all stories are erased.
**Rivals:** The Holy Cult.

### 3. The Forgetting
**Type:** Sentient Constructs / Memory Erasers
**Leader:** The First Forgetting, a being who seeks to erase all memories from existence.
**Asset:** **Memory Erasure:** They can erase memories from living beings, leaving them as blank slates.
**Rivals:** The Holy Cult.

### 4. The Echo-Weavers
**Type:** Primal Rulers / Elemental Embodiments
**Leader:** The Lord of Echoes, a colossal elemental of pure sound.
**Asset:** **Raw Echo Power:** They are the embodiment of pure sound, capable of unleashing devastating sound-based attacks.
**Rivals:** The Nihilists.

### 5. The Dogma Thieves
**Type:** Criminal Syndicate / Belief Spies
**Leader:** The Master Thief, a master of disguise who can steal any dogma.
**Asset:** **Dogma-Jars:** Jars that can extract a dogma directly from a being's mind.
**Rivals:** The Holy Cult.

### 6. The Essence of Unity Keepers
**Type:** Mystic Order / Emotional Harmonizers
**Leader:** The Harmonious Divine, a being who seeks to balance all emotions.
**Asset:** **Emotional Auras:** They can create auras that balance and enhance the emotions of any being.
**Rivals:** The Nihilists.

### 7. The Divine Spark Seekers
**Type:** Adventurers / Rebirth Hunters
**Leader:** The Immortal Divine, a legendary figure who seeks the Recipe of Rebirth.
**Asset:** **Faith Maps:** They possess ancient maps that lead to rumored Divine Spark fragments.
**Rivals:** The Holy Cult (who try to regulate their activities).

### 8. The Ash-Weavers Guild
**Type:** Artisans / Construct Builders
**Leader:** Master Ash-Weaver Borin, a dwarf who can build golems from pure ash.
**Asset:** **Ash Golems:** Golems made of pure ash, capable of unleashing devastating ash-based attacks.
**Rivals:** The Forgetting.

### 9. The Divine Spark's Chosen
**Type:** Divine Servants / Faith Guardians
**Leader:** The Divine Spark, a being whose touch can instantly restore any lost dogma.
**Asset:** **Divine Faith:** They can instantly restore any lost dogma to its original state.
**Rivals:** The Nihilists.

### 10. The Fury-Eaters
**Type:** Necromancers / Emotional Vampires
**Leader:** The Lord of Fury, a being who feeds on fury.
**Asset:** **Fury-Drain Rituals:** They can drain the fury from living beings, gaining temporary power.
**Rivals:** The Essence of Unity Keepers.

### Subsystem: Faith Balance
*   **Balance Score (0-10):** Represents the emotional balance of the current zone. Starts at 5.
*   **Gaining Balance:** Restoring corrupted dogma, creating harmonious emotions, performing "balanced" actions.
*   **Losing Balance:** Creating chaotic emotions, succumbing to the Divine Spark's fury, experiencing prolonged emotional instability.
*   **Effects:**
    *   *High Balance:* Emotions are stable, predictable. Spells work as intended.
    *   *Low Balance:* Emotions are unstable, unpredictable. Spells can backfire, physics glitches.
    *   *0 Balance:* The zone collapses into a single, undifferentiated emotion.

### Subsystem: Faith-Weaving
*   **Faith-Weaving:** Magic is treated as manipulating the divine dust of lost things. Spells are "rituals."
*   **Fury Risks:** If a spellcaster rolls a natural 1 on a spell attack or saving throw, they cause a "Fury Surge." The spell backfires, or a minor emotional glitch occurs in the simulation.
*   **Particle Injection:** Faith-Weavers can attempt to "inject" custom particles into the simulation, creating unique effects. This requires a successful INT (Faith-Weaving) check, with the DC determined by the complexity of the desired effect.

# Blades of the Holy War

*A Crusades Campaign — Long Campaign (12-14 sessions) — Medium-High difficulty*

---

## Campaign Overview

**Title:** Blades of the Holy War
**Campaign Type / Genre:** Holy War / Cultural Clash / Moral Ambiguity
**Estimated Length:** Long Campaign — 12-14 sessions
**Player Level Range:** Start **3** → Finish **10**
**Tone (keywords):** zealous, complex, brutal, honorable, tragic
**Core Premise (2–4 sentences):**
The players are crusaders who answered the Pope's call to reclaim the Holy Land, finding themselves in a world where religious conviction clashes with political reality, where "infidels" prove more honorable than fellow Christians, and where the noble goal of pilgrimage transforms into conquest and atrocity. As knights, priests, merchants, and idealists, they must navigate the impossible contradictions between faith and greed, holy war and unholy conduct, and their romantic vision of crusade versus its brutal reality. The campaign explores religious warfare from both sides, the tragedy of cultures that might have coexisted instead warring, and whether any war can truly be holy. This is a story of the Crusades—where heroes must choose between their faith and their honor.

---

## Story Arc (Long Campaign — 12-14 sessions)

A journey from religious fervor to moral complexity.

*   **Act I — The Call (Sessions 1–4):** Answering the crusade and learning war's reality.
*   **Act II — The Holy Land (Sessions 5–10):** Siege of Jerusalem and moral reckoning.
*   **Act III — After the Conquest (Sessions 11–14):** Living with what was done in God's name.

---

## Session-by-Session Outline

### Session 1 — "Deus Vult"
*   **Focus:** Introduction / Religious Fervor
*   **Key Events:**
    *   Players witness Pope Urban II's call for crusade to liberate Jerusalem from "infidel occupation."
    *   Thousands take the cross, seeing it as pilgrimage, adventure, penance, and holy duty.
    *   The crusade begins with genuine religious fervor and romantic idealism.
**Escalation:** Reality sets in quickly—the journey is brutal, supplies fail, and allied Byzantine Christians prove untrustworthy.

### Sessions 2-3 — "The People's Crusade"
*   **Focus:** First Horrors / Shattered Ideals
*   **Key Events:**
    *   The undisciplined People's Crusade massacres Jewish communities en route, claiming they're fighting God's enemies.
    *   Players must decide: participate, prevent, or look away from these atrocities.
    *   The People's Crusade is annihilated by Turkish forces—peasant zealots against professional soldiers.
**Escalation:** The main crusader armies arrive and players witness the political maneuvering—this is as much conquest as pilgrimage.

### Sessions 4-5 — "The Siege of Antioch"
*   **Focus:** Siege Warfare / Desperation
*   **Key Events:**
    *   The crusaders besiege Antioch for eight months, enduring starvation, disease, and Turkish counterattacks.
    *   Players experience the horror of medieval siege warfare from the attacker's perspective.
    *   When Antioch falls through betrayal, the crusaders massacre the population despite surrender promises.
**Escalation:** The crusaders themselves are besieged in Antioch. Players witness "miraculous" morale restoration through religious fervor.

### Sessions 6-7 — "The March to Jerusalem"
*   **Focus:** Pilgrimage / Military Campaign
*   **Key Events:**
    *   The crusade advances toward Jerusalem, treating it as both holy pilgrimage and military conquest.
    *   Players encounter Saracen warriors who fight with honor and Muslim scholars of great learning.
    *   The moral complexity becomes undeniable—the "infidels" are not the monsters they expected.
**Escalation:** Jerusalem is sighted. The crusaders prepare for the culminating siege that will define the crusade.

### Sessions 8-9 — "The Fall of Jerusalem"
*   **Focus:** Climactic Siege / Moral Horror
*   **Key Events:**
    *   The crusaders assault Jerusalem's walls in fierce fighting where both sides believe God is with them.
    *   The city falls. Historical accounts describe crusaders wading through blood up to their ankles.
    *   Players must choose their participation in massacre that follows—are they crusaders or murderers?
**Escalation:** Jerusalem is won. The crusade succeeded. But players must reckon with what victory cost.

### Sessions 10-11 — "The Kingdom of Heaven"
*   **Focus:** Aftermath / Complexity
*   **Key Events:**
    *   Crusader kingdoms are established in the Holy Land, requiring governance not just conquest.
    *   Players meet Saladin, the Muslim leader whose honor and nobility challenge every crusader assumption.
    *   Some crusaders integrate with local culture; others remain militant occupiers.
**Escalation:** Saladin unites Muslim forces to reclaim Jerusalem. The crusaders must defend what they took.

### Sessions 12-13 — "The Horns of Hattin"
*   **Focus:** Legendary Battle / Reversal
*   **Key Events:**
    *   Saladin outmaneuvers the crusader army at Hattin, destroying Christian military power.
    *   Players participate in the legendary clash between Richard Lionheart and Saladin—enemies who respect each other.
    *   The crusade ends not in eternal Christian control but in negotiated pilgrimage rights.
**Escalation:** Players realize the Crusades accomplished little permanent except teaching Christians and Muslims to hate each other.

### Session 14 — "The True Cross"
*   **Focus:** Resolution / Legacy
*   **Key Events:**
    *   Players return to Europe after years abroad, changed by what they experienced.
    *   They must decide what to tell people: the romantic crusade narrative or the complex reality.
    *   The campaign ends asking: Was it a holy war or a tragic mistake? Both? Neither?
**Escalation:** The epilogue shows later crusades repeating the same cycles—zeal, brutality, complexity, failure.

## Central Conflict

This campaign explores religious warfare, cultural clash, and the impossibility of truly holy war. The central conflict is the players' journey from crusading fervor to moral complexity as they encounter honorable enemies, witness crusader atrocities, and realize that both sides believe God is with them. They must navigate between genuine faith and political exploitation of religion, between crusading brotherhood and cynical conquest, and between viewing opponents as inhuman infidels or recognizing shared humanity. The campaign asks: Can war ever be holy? How does religious conviction justify violence? What happens when the enemy proves more honorable than your allies? And can people of different faiths coexist or must they war? The answer: the Crusades taught that mixing religious zealotry with violence creates tragedy—regardless of which side claims God's blessing.

## Unique Mechanics

### 1. **Faith vs. Reality Tracker**
*   Measures the gap between crusade ideals and crusader actions.
*   As atrocities mount, this gap widens, challenging players' faith justifications.
*   Players must reconcile what they believe with what they witness and do.

### 2. **Cultural Understanding**
*   Track players' recognition of Muslim culture, honor, and shared humanity.
*   Higher understanding creates moral complexity and opportunities for diplomacy.
*   Low understanding maintains simple crusade narrative but limits options.

### 3. **Chivalric Honor**
*   Medieval honor code that applies regardless of opponent's religion.
*   Players can choose honor (treating enemies with respect) or zealotry (anything is permitted against infidels).
*   Their choices determine whether they're knights or fanatics.

### 4. **The Three Perspectives**
*   Scenes are shown from Crusader, Muslim, and local Christian perspectives.
*   This reinforces that all sides believe they're fighting for justice and God.
*   Players see the tragedy of unnecessary war between faiths with more commonality than difference.

### 5. **Legacy of Understanding**
*   Track moments where crusaders and Muslims recognize shared humanity.
*   These moments create alternative history possibilities—what if they'd cooperated?
*   The epilogue shows whether players helped create mutual understanding or deepened religious hatred.
