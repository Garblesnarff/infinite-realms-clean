# Fate's Warning: Campaign Bible

## [TAG: LORE_CORE]
### The Nature of Magic
Magic in this world is not a public spectacle; it is a hidden, dangerous layer of reality overlaying modern London. To the uninitiated "Sleepers," it doesn't exist. To Mages, it is the defining force of their lives. Magic is divided into specific types (Life, Fire, Force, Time, Space, Mind, etc.), and a mage can only master one type. This specialization creates a rock-paper-scissors dynamic in combat—a Fire Mage can incinerate a room, but a Mind Mage can make them forget how to light a match.
*   **Divination:** The rarest and often most undervalued magic. Diviners cannot throw fireballs or heal wounds. Instead, they see "Probability Paths"—ghostly, branching futures. In combat, they don't react; they pre-act, dodging a punch before it's thrown. In politics, they see betrayal before the knife is drawn. It is a lonely, wearying magic, forcing the user to witness a thousand tragedies for every one they prevent.
*   **The Light and The Dark:** These are political labels, not moral ones. "Light Mages" join the Council, follow rules, and believe in order and hierarchy. They are often politicians, bureaucrats, and enforcers who are just as ruthless as their counterparts. "Dark Mages" are those who refuse to join, valuing personal power and freedom above all else. They are not necessarily evil, but they are dangerous and unregulated.
*   **Gatekeepers and Elsewhere:** Mages can create "Elsewhere"—pocket dimensions that exist outside normal space. These range from simple storage closets to vast, personal fortresses. To enter, one usually needs a "Gatestone" or permission from the creator.

### Timeline
1.  **The Concordat (c. 1950s):** After a devastating magical war that nearly exposed them to the Sleepers, the strongest mages formed the Light Council. They established a system of laws to police the magical community and ensure secrecy.
2.  **The Purge (c. 1980s):** Under the guise of "public safety," the Council launched a brutal campaign to eradicate or forcibly recruit independent Dark Mages. Many were killed; others, like Richard Drakh, went into hiding, nurturing deep grudges.
3.  **The Apprentice's Escape (10 Years Ago):** The protagonist (the "Diviner") was an apprentice to Richard Drakh. Realizing Drakh viewed them only as a tool for a future catastrophe, they fled. They spent a decade building a quiet, neutral life as a shopkeeper, balancing on the razor's edge between Light and Dark.
4.  **The Return of the Shadow (Present Day):** Richard Drakh re-emerges. He is no longer hiding. He has found a way to access a power source that could reshape reality, and he needs his former apprentice—specifically their ability to navigate probability—to retrieve it.
5.  **The Council's Ultimatum (Present Day):** Terrified of Drakh, the Council demands the Diviner's allegiance. They offer "protection" which looks suspiciously like imprisonment. The Diviner is caught between a master who wants to use them and a government that wants to cage them.

### The "Why"
The central conflict is about the burden of knowledge and the illusion of choice. The players are not heroes saving the world from a cackling villain; they are survivors trying to navigate a maze where every turn leads to pain. The "Why" is the question: "If you can see a future where you die, and a future where you kill an innocent to survive, which do you choose?" It explores the corruption of institutions (The Council) versus the terrifying anarchy of absolute freedom (Drakh).

## [TAG: FACTION_DATA]
### The Light Council
**Type:** Magical Government/Bureaucracy
**Leader:** The Senior Council, a rotating body of the most politically powerful mages in Britain. Currently dominated by Levistus.
**Asset:** The "Keepers," an elite police force of combat mages, and total control over the magical economy and legal system.
**Rivals:** Dark Mages, Independents, anyone who threatens the status quo.

### Dark Mages
**Type:** Loose Confederation of Individualists
**Leader:** None formally, though Richard Drakh commands fear and respect.
**Asset:** Raw, forbidden power. They practice magic the Council deems "unethical" or "too dangerous," giving them an edge in raw lethality.
**Rivals:** The Light Council.

### The Independents
**Type:** Survivalists and Outcasts
**Leader:** None. They are defined by their refusal to join a side.
**Asset:** Anonymity and niche skills. They are the smugglers, information brokers, and fixers of the magical world. The players likely start here.
**Rivals:** Both Light and Dark Mages pressure them to pick a side.

### The Adepts
**Type:** Sensitive Humans
**Leader:** None.
**Asset:** Minor magical talents (seeing ghosts, finding lost objects, slight luck). They are the underclass of the magical world, often employed as assistants or pawns by mages.
**Rivals:** Mages who exploit them.

## [TAG: NPC_TIER_1]
**Richard Drakh** (Dark Mage - Destruction) - The former master. Cold, pragmatic, and terrifyingly powerful. **Voice:** Calm, cultured, almost fatherly, even when threatening death. **Goal:** To break the Council and rewrite the laws of magic. **Secret:** He genuinely cares for his apprentice in a twisted, possessive way, viewing them as his greatest creation.
**Luna** (Independent Mage - Chance) - A chaotic ally. Her magic manipulates luck and probability, but it always demands a price (bad luck later). **Voice:** Fast-paced, nervous, with a dark sense of humor. **Goal:** To survive the coming war without owing anyone a favor. **Secret:** She is cursed; her "bad luck" backlash inevitably hurts the people she loves, not her.
**Anne** (Light Mage - Life) - A Council healer with a conscience. She believes in the system but hates the corruption. **Voice:** Gentle, firm, and tired. **Goal:** To protect the innocent, even if it means defying the Council. **Secret:** She has been secretly healing Dark Mages who were brutally hunted by Keepers, keeping a ledger of the Council's crimes.
**Levistus** (Light Mage - Mind) - A high-ranking Council politician. Slippery, manipulative, and obsessed with order. **Voice:** Smooth, persuasive, and patronizing. **Goal:** To capture Drakh and consolidate total power over the UK's mages. **Secret:** He is terrified that he is actually weaker than Drakh and is bluffing with the entire might of the Council.
**Arachne** (Ancient Entity - Crafter) - A giant, intelligent spider-being who weaves magical items in her cave. Neutral and ancient. **Voice:** Multi-tonal, clicking, and ancient. **Goal:** To create the perfect weave of fate. **Secret:** She can see the "Probability Paths" even better than the Diviner, but she refuses to intervene, viewing history as a tapestry to be observed, not altered.

## [TAG: LOCATIONS_MAIN]
### The Arcana Emporium
A small, cluttered magic shop in Camden Market. To Sleepers, it sells tarot cards and crystals. To Mages, it is a neutral ground and a repository of rare artifacts.
*   **The Front Room:** Smells of incense and old paper. Filled with "tourist trap" items.
*   **The Back Room:** Protected by wards. Contains real grimoires and a scrying pool.
*   **The Living Quarters:** A cozy, lived-in flat above the shop. The Diviner's sanctuary.

### The Council Chambers
Located beneath the Houses of Parliament, accessible only by a specific magical elevator.
*   **The Rotunda:** A grand, circular debating hall. Smells of polished wood and beeswax.
*   **The Holding Cells:** Damp, magic-suppressing cells for rogue mages.
*   **Levistus's Office:** Minimalist, cold, and intimidating.

### The Shadow Realm
A dimension of living darkness used for travel and hiding, but dangerous to traverse.
*   **The Obsidian Path:** A safe(ish) walkway through the swirling dark.
*   **The Whispering Void:** Areas where the shadows try to speak to you.
*   **The Mirror Lake:** A pool of darkness that reflects your worst possible future.

### Arachne's Cave
A pocket dimension accessible through a specific crack in a London Underground tunnel.
*   **The Web:** A massive, glowing web where Arachne sits.
*   **The Loom:** Where she weaves magical cloth.
*   **The Trophy Room:** Where she keeps the gear of mages who tried to attack her.

## [TAG: QUEST_MAIN]
1.  **The Shopkeeper:** The campaign begins with a "normal" day at the Arcana Emporium. The player must deal with a difficult customer (a disguised Dark Mage) and use divination to prevent a minor disaster.
2.  **The Warning:** Richard Drakh sends a message—a cursed object that shows a vision of London burning. He demands a meeting.
3.  **The Council's Knock:** Keeper Stroud arrives to "question" the player about Drakh's return. The player must navigate the interrogation without getting arrested.
4.  **The First Choice:** The player must choose whether to meet Drakh, go to the Council for help (risky), or run.
5.  **The Heist:** Drakh forces/convinces the player to help break into a Council vault to steal a "Gate Key." This requires using precognition to bypass security systems.
6.  **The Betrayal:** During the heist, it's revealed that the Council was using the Key to weaponize Adepts. Anne (the Life Mage) asks for help exposing this.
7.  **The Shadow Realm Run:** The player must flee through the Shadow Realm to escape pursuing Keepers.
8.  **Arachne's Bargain:** The player seeks refuge with Arachne. She offers a weapon capable of stopping Drakh, but demands a terrible price (a specific memory or future possibility).
9.  **The War Begins:** Drakh launches an open attack on the Council. London becomes a battlefield. The player sees multiple futures where everyone dies.
10. **The Third Path:** The player must find a "probability path" that isn't total destruction or total tyranny. This involves manipulating both Drakh and Levistus into a trap.
11. **The Final Confrontation:** A three-way standoff in a pocket dimension. The player uses their mastery of probability to force a stalemate or a specific outcome.

## [TAG: ITEM_LEGENDARY]
### 1. The Monkey's Paw (Real)
**Type:** Cursed Artifact
**Description:** A withered, mummified paw. It radiates malice.
**Mechanics:** Grants 3 wishes (casting *Wish*). However, the DM *must* twist the wish in the most horrific, ironic way possible based on the "Probability Path" not taken.

### 2. The Weaver's Silk
**Type:** Armor (Light)
**Description:** A vest woven by Arachne from shadow and spider silk.
**Mechanics:** +2 AC. The wearer can cast *Misty Step* as a reaction when attacked. It feels like thousands of tiny legs on your skin.

### 3. Drakh's Focus
**Type:** Wand/Focus
**Description:** A shard of obsidian that seems to absorb light.
**Mechanics:** +2 to spell attack rolls. Once per day, the user can maximize all damage dice on a Necromancy spell, but takes 1d8 necrotic damage per spell level.

(CR 3). Humanoid (Mage). HP 40, AC 15 (Mage Armor).
**Abilities:**
*   **Force Bolt:** Ranged spell attack. 1d10 force damage.

(CR 4). Monstrosity. HP 60, AC 14.
**Abilities:**
*   **Amorphous:** Can move through a space as narrow as 1 inch.
*   **Strength Drain:** Melee attack. 2d6 necrotic damage, target's STR score is reduced by 1d4.
*   **Tactics:** Ambush predators from the Shadow Realm.

(CR 5). Humanoid (Mage). HP 50, AC 16 (Precognition).
**Abilities:**
*   **Probability Dodge:** Uses Reaction to impose Disadvantage on an attack roll.
*   **Guided Strike:** Attacks have Advantage.
*   **Tactics:** Annoying to hit. Will predict your moves and counter-attack.

## [TAG: RULES_CUSTOM]
### Probability Mechanics
*   **Precognition:** The Diviner player can spend a resource (Spell Slot or "Focus Point") to ask the DM: "What happens if I do X?" The DM must give a truthful, short vision of the immediate consequence.
*   **The "Bad Choice" Bonus:** If the player knowingly chooses a "bad" path (one that leads to injury or complication) because it serves the greater good/story, they gain "Fate Points" (Inspiration) to use later.

## [TAG: DM_GUIDE]
*   **Tone:** Keep it grounded. Even when throwing fireballs, worry about collateral damage and CCTV cameras.
*   **Divination:** This is hard to DM. Don't plan linear plots. Plan situations and let the player "see" the outcomes of their choices. Be ready to improvise based on which "path" they choose.
*   **Morality:** Neither side is good. The Council provides order but is fascist. Drakh offers freedom but is a terrorist. Make the players uncomfortable with both allies.
