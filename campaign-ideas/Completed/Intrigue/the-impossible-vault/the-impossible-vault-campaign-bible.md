# Campaign Bible: The Impossible Vault

## Section 1: DEEP LORE & HISTORY
[TAG: LORE_CORE]

### Origin Myth: The Probability Anchor
The Grand Celestial Casino is not a building that was built so much as a wound that was furnished. Beneath its marble and neon-algae lies the Probability Anchor, a scar in reality where the Goddess of Fortune and the God of Entropy once fought a duel that neither of them, by every account, actually wanted to win. Their blood mingled in the sand and never fully separated again, and where it pooled, luck stopped being a feeling and became a liquid: Chance-Matter, viscous and gold-bright, pumped through the Casino's walls the way water moves through any other city's pipes. The house always wins because the architecture is drinking. Every chip flipped, every wheel spun, every held breath at a high-stakes table feeds a little of itself into the walls, and the walls feed the city's lights, and the city calls this progress. Somewhere in the paperwork, nobody ever agreed to the arrangement. Nobody ever does.

### Historical Timeline
1.  **The Fracture (pre-history):** The duel cracks the desert floor open. For a generation afterward, dice thrown anywhere within three days' ride of the site land on whichever face the thrower needed most, whether or not they deserved it.
2.  **The First Bet (300 years ago):** A nameless wanderer bets a passing wind spirit for the rights to the cracked land and wins on a coin that hadn't finished falling. The foundation stone of the Casino is laid on the spot where the coin finally landed, eleven days later.
3.  **The Goldweaver Ascension (150 years ago):** The Goldweaver family learns to refine raw Chance-Matter into stable fuel, and Glimmerfall stops being a mining camp and starts being a city with streetlights that never flicker — except, everyone quietly agrees not to mention, on nights when someone loses everything at the tables.
4.  **The Glinting Plague (100 years ago):** A contagion turns dozens of citizens into solid gold, mid-gesture, mid-sentence. Lord Midas's grandfather sells a "cure" that is really a stalling agent; the statues that didn't get dosed in time still stand in the Sky-Garden, and the family fortune was never really about gold. It was about being the only ones who understood what gold was for.
5.  **The Architecture War (80 years ago):** Euclidean mages and their non-Euclidean rivals fight over the Casino's expansion rights. The non-Euclidean side wins, which is why the City Archives occasionally relocate a reading room overnight and nobody has ever successfully complained to management about it.
6.  **The Ashcombe Reckoning (20 years ago):** A reformer named Edric Ashcombe tries to convince the city that Chance-Matter belongs to everyone who generates it, not just the house that collects it. He is quietly framed for "metaphysical embezzlement," and the Specialists' Guild broker who sold the tip that sank him — a gnome who has never once raised his voice since — still runs the Guild's ledger today. Edric's son, Darian, grew up being told his father was a thief. He has spent every year since learning otherwise.
7.  **The Silence of the Slots (10 years ago):** Every machine in the city stops for exactly one hour. Four hundred people, mid-bet, mid-sentence, mid-breath, simply are not there anymore. The official record calls it a localized Chance-Matter discharge and moves on. The truth, known to perhaps three people alive, is worse and stranger: Lord Midas Goldweaver banked them. Four hundred living people, converted into four hundred chips and racked in a spiral at the bottom of the Vault of Stars, still aware, still dreaming, still faintly warm. One of them is a reformer's father. One of them is a security officer's little sister, a girl named Posy Nightingale, banked at eleven years old and still, somewhere in the dark, eleven.
8.  **The Neon Reformation (5 years ago):** Bioluminescent algae replaces the city's old gaslamps, and Glimmerfall gets a lighting grid that changes color with the collective mood of everyone standing under it. On the anniversary of the Silence every year, without anyone programming it to, the whole grid dims for exactly one hour.
9.  **The Weeping Ace (ongoing):** No one can say precisely when the card started appearing, only that it has never stopped. It shows a single face, mid-tear, and it turns up somewhere in the city roughly once a session — dealt into an unrelated hand, folded on a napkin, staring back from a fogged mirror. It is not a curse. It is four hundred people trying to knock on a door they no longer have hands to knock on. Whoever receives it gets a fragment of truth, and the house, somehow, always notices.
10. **The Tournament Decree (present day):** Lord Goldweaver announces the Tournament of Eclipses, a season of high-stakes matches timed to culminate the night of the solar eclipse. The city calls it a celebration. It is, in fact, a very expensive way of making sure four hundred thousand eyes are on the arena and none of them are on the vault beneath it.

### The "Why"
Lord Midas Goldweaver is not hoarding gold. He gave up caring about gold the day his left hand turned to it — slowly, permanently, and against his will, a poisoning that began the moment he first banked a living soul into Chance-Matter and has been creeping toward his heart ever since. He wears gloves now, always, and tells the Committee it is a fashion choice. What he is actually hoarding is a cure, and the cure, he has convinced himself, is godhood: on the night of the eclipse, in a ritual he calls the Eclipse Draw, he intends to drain the Anchor and every banked chip in the Vault straight into himself, becoming a living reservoir of luck itself. He believes this will stop the gold. It will not. It will simply mean the entire city inherits his condition — permanent, structural misfortune, crops failing, dice turning against everyone, forever — while the four hundred people in the Vault are erased past any hope of recovery, their chips spent like any other currency.

He is not alone in the mirror. Darian Ashcombe, the mastermind pulling together the crew that will try to stop this, carries his father's stolen chip against his own skin, and it has been paying him back for the theft one grain at a time: his blood has started turning to gold dust, visible now only at the wrist, and he has told no one but Lyra. Two men are killing themselves on the same luck, from opposite ends of the same theft, and neither one has said so out loud. The Eleventh — the last hire, the one Darian insists the plan cannot work without — is the only person in Glimmerfall who doesn't yet know that everyone at this table is already dying of the thing they're stealing.

## Section 2: FACTIONS
[TAG: FACTION_DATA]

### 1. The Casino Security Consortium
**Type:** Corporate Paramilitary Order
**Leader:** Commander Vex, Half-Orc Paladin sworn to an Oath of order.
**Asset:** The Panopticon Engine, a crystal eye mounted above the Gaming Floor that sees five seconds into the future, giving security a permanent, narrow head start.
**Rivals:** The Underworld Alliance, The Society of the Folded Hand.
**Note:** Vex has quietly doubled patrols on a sub-basement corridor nobody assigned him to guard, because some part of him has never believed the official story about the Silence of the Slots.

### 2. The Underworld Alliance
**Type:** Criminal Syndicate and Merchant Guild
**Leader:** Madame Fortuna, Tiefling Warlock.
**Asset:** The Sewers of Whispers, a tunnel network that carries every secret spoken near a grate to whoever knows how to listen for it.
**Rivals:** The Casino Security Consortium, The Accountants of Zero.
**Note:** She has been in love with Commander Vex for eleven years and has told him precisely never, on the grounds that it would be bad for both their businesses.

### 3. The Specialists' Guild
**Type:** Unionized Freelance Underworld
**Leader:** Quillon Marsh, a gnome broker whose whole professional gift is being forgettable.
**Asset:** A ledger that can locate any freelance talent in the city within the hour, for a fee that always feels fair until the job is finished.
**Rivals:** unlicensed amateurs undercutting Guild rates.
**Note:** Twenty years ago he sold out a reformer named Edric Ashcombe for less coin than he'd charge today to source a single lockpick. He has never told anyone, and the reformer's son now sits across from him at the negotiating table roughly once a year without either of them saying his father's name.

### 4. The Accountants of Zero
**Type:** Mathematical Zealots
**Leader:** Auditor Null, Human Monk.
**Asset:** The Void-Ledgers, books that erase a name's fortune from the world's ledger of luck when written in the correct hand, leaving the target statistically, metaphysically average for the rest of their life.
**Rivals:** everyone who has ever profited from chance, which in Glimmerfall is everyone.
**Note:** They believe every jackpot is a theft from some stranger who will never know they were owed the difference, and they have appointed themselves that stranger's unpaid collection agency.

### 5. The Neon Saints
**Type:** Aesthetic Paladin Order
**Leader:** Lumen Halcyra, Celestialborn Paladin.
**Asset:** Control of the city's bioluminescent light grid; they can dim Glimmerfall to a heartbeat of glow or burn it blinding, and everyone in the city has learned to read the color of the streetlamps like weather.
**Rivals:** The Velvet Cord.
**Note:** Lumen took her lighting oath the same year the Slots went silent, and has never once explained why to anyone who asked.

### 6. The Velvet Cord
**Type:** Elitist Secret Society
**Leader:** Corwin Ashcombe, a Human noble who has not spoken his own family name aloud in nineteen years and wears a mirrored mask so no one can make him.
**Asset:** Absolute social immunity inside the Casino; house guards are bound by old favors not to see a Cord member commit so much as a faux pas, let alone a crime.
**Rivals:** The Specialists' Guild, who make a comfortable living stealing Cord invitations.
**Note:** Corwin is Darian's older brother, and sided with Midas the year their father was framed. He believes, to this day, that he chose the winning side.

### 7. The Second-Second
**Type:** Chronomancer Cheaters' Circle
**Leader:** Hourglass Harry, Satyr Wizard.
**Asset:** Watches that rewind the wearer exactly two seconds — just long enough to change a bet, take back a word, or catch a card before it's seen.
**Rivals:** every pit boss in the city, who have all, by now, learned to hate the particular click those watches make.
**Note:** Harry has rewound the same two seconds of his life more than four hundred times, on four hundred different nights, and has never once used the trick to fix the thing he actually regrets.

### 8. The Society of the Folded Hand
**Type:** Rogue Philosophers of Chance
**Leader:** a standing council of three masked philosophers who speak only in strict turn, never interrupting, never agreeing outright.
**Asset:** Paper golems built from losing lottery tickets, animated by the compressed grief of everyone who lost that particular hand.
**Rivals:** The Casino Security Consortium, who consider them a public nuisance bordering on a fire hazard.
**Note:** They fold a Weeping Ace into the chest of every golem they build and will not say why, except that it "feels correct," which is the closest thing the Society has to a religious position.

### 9. The Chip Widows
**Type:** Grief Society, Potential Allies
**Leader:** none formally; the loudest griever in a given week tends to be listened to.
**Asset:** The largest living memory of the Silence of the Slots left in Glimmerfall. They know every one of the four hundred names, in order, and recite them once a year whether anyone is listening or not.
**Rivals:** The Eclipse Committee.
**Note:** Every anniversary, they leave a single folded Weeping Ace at every door the Casino owns. The house always has it removed before dawn. They always leave another one the next year, and the year after that, and have never once missed.

### 10. The Eclipse Committee
**Type:** Tournament Bookmakers, Midas's Creatures
**Leader:** unnamed and plural; no two people can agree they've met the same Committee member twice.
**Asset:** The Tournament of Eclipses itself, rigged match by rigged match to keep the entire city's attention fixed on the arena and away from the vault humming quietly beneath it.
**Rivals:** anyone who asks, even once, who actually owns the betting lines.
**Note:** Every Committee member wears house gold from wrist to knuckle, and not one of them has ever been seen without their gloves on.

## Section 3: NPC ROSTER
[TAG: NPC_TIER_1]

### Major NPCs
1.  **Darian Ashcombe** (Human Bard) - The mastermind behind the job, known across three districts by the nickname Silvertongue; charming, melancholic, quietly vengeful about a father the city was taught to call a thief. **Voice:** smooth baritone, speaks in metaphors, takes a deliberate pause before answering anything important. **Goal:** recover the Anchor Key and clear his father's name. **Secret:** the chip he carries against his skin is turning his blood to gold dust, one grain at a time.
2.  **Lyra Nightingale** (Half-Elf Rogue) - Head of Casino surveillance and the crew's inside woman, called Whisper by everyone who's worked with her twice; stoic, precise, exhausted in a way she never explains. **Voice:** low, crisp, almost monotone until something she cares about is threatened. **Goal:** get her little sister's chip out of the Vault alive. **Secret:** she designed the Vault's fatal trap herself, and Midas knows it, which is the leash he holds her by.
3.  **Jax Tumbler** (Gnome Rogue) - The crew's Lock, small and endlessly curious, first cracked the Vault's outer door as a boy on a dare and has sworn ever since that it let him go on purpose. **Voice:** rapid-fire, skittish, punctuated by little satisfied clicks of the tongue. **Goal:** open the one door that's never let anyone in twice. **Secret:** he still hears the Vault "breathing" some nights, and he's never told the crew he goes back to listen.
4.  **Sylas Marrow** (Tiefling Bard) - The crew's Face, a master of disguise magic who has worn so many other people's features that he genuinely isn't certain, some mornings, which one is his. **Voice:** fluid, matches whoever he's speaking to a beat too well. **Goal:** pull off one perfect impersonation and retire before he loses the original. **Secret:** he keeps a single portrait hidden in his coat, the only proof of what he actually looks like.
5.  **Zara Emberkettle** (Dwarf Wizard) - The crew's Boom, a transmutation specialist with a gift for controlled demolition and a laugh that carries across three rooms. **Voice:** loud, hearty, blunt about everything except one thing. **Goal:** engineer the single biggest, cleanest detonation of her career. **Secret:** she has been secretly, thoroughly afraid of fire since a childhood accident she has never once mentioned to anyone in the crew.
6.  **Kael Stillwater** (Elf Ranger) - The crew's Hawk, blind since a hunting accident and seeing the world entirely through the eyes of his hawk familiar, S'kree. **Voice:** barely speaks above a murmur, prefers hand signs the crew has learned to read. **Goal:** land the one shot that proves blindness was never the disability everyone assumed. **Secret:** if S'kree is ever killed or captured, Kael is not merely blind — he is alone in the dark for the first time in a decade.
7.  **Luna Varga** (Human Rogue) - The crew's Drive, cocky and quick behind the reins of an enchanted carriage that has never once failed to outrun anything. **Voice:** fast, slangy, punctuated with engine-noise impressions she can't help making. **Goal:** finish one last job so she can finally slow down. **Secret:** the carriage's uncanny instincts are her late grandmother's spirit, still riding along in the engine, still backseat-driving every turn.
8.  **Orion Vey** (Astralborn Wizard) - The crew's Mind, a divination specialist whose visions arrive with splitting headaches and worse timing. **Voice:** distant, dreamy, trails off mid-sentence when a new vision surfaces. **Goal:** finally act on a warning instead of just recording it. **Secret:** he foresaw a fragment of the Silence of the Slots ten years ago and dismissed it as noise; he has never forgiven himself.
9.  **Brutus Hale** (Half-Orc Fighter) - The crew's Brick, enormous, gentle, and startlingly well-read, wants nothing more from this last job than enough coin to buy a flower shop. **Voice:** deep, slow, unexpectedly eloquent. **Goal:** walk away from violence for good, on his own terms, with the money to do it. **Secret:** he has never told the crew he already has a shopfront picked out, three streets from the Chapel, because saying it out loud feels like tempting the luck he's spent a lifetime distrusting.
10. **Trixie Quickfoot** (Halfling Bard) - The crew's Shuffle, all sleight of hand and misdirection, keeps the mood light because someone in this crew has to. **Voice:** singsong, quick, laughs a half-second before the joke lands. **Goal:** protect the family betting stall the Eclipse Committee has been quietly squeezing out of business. **Secret:** she is the only crew member who has already lost something to this heist before it started, and she has told no one how much the stall means to her.
11. **Lord Midas Goldweaver** (Elf Wizard) - Owner of the Grand Celestial Casino, paranoid, twitchy, and quietly terrified beneath the theatrical arrogance. **Voice:** high, fast, interrupts his own sentences. **Goal:** complete the Eclipse Draw and become a living reservoir of luck. **Secret:** his left hand is solid gold and the gold is creeping toward his heart; he has also, somewhere along the way, forgotten his own birth name.
12. **Commander Vex** (Half-Orc Paladin) - Head of Casino security, rigid, literal, unfailingly correct in every particular except the one that matters. **Voice:** clipped, formal, no contractions. **Goal:** maintain order inside the Casino at any cost. **Secret:** he has never believed the official account of the Silence of the Slots, and has spent ten years quietly, uselessly investigating it alone.
13. **Madame Fortuna** (Tiefling Warlock) - Head of the Underworld Alliance, sultry, dangerous, and unexpectedly maternal toward the people who work for her. **Voice:** smoky, unhurried, draws out her vowels. **Goal:** expand her territory without starting a war she can't win. **Secret:** she is in love with Commander Vex and has decided, permanently, never to act on it.
14. **Quillon Marsh** (Gnome Rogue) - Broker for the Specialists' Guild, deliberately plain, deliberately forgettable. **Voice:** aggressively average, mid-range, the kind of voice you forget while he's still talking. **Goal:** keep the Guild profitable and himself invisible. **Secret:** he sold out Darian's father twenty years ago for a sum he wouldn't blink at charging for a single lockpick today.
15. **Auditor Null** (Human Monk) - Leader of the Accountants of Zero, cold, serene, utterly convinced that luck itself is a moral failing. **Voice:** flat, measures out sentences like currency. **Goal:** balance every unearned fortune in the city back to zero. **Secret:** he keeps a single lucky coin in his robes that he has never once turned in to his own ledgers.
16. **Corwin Ashcombe** (Human Rogue) - Masked leader of the Velvet Cord, Darian's estranged older brother, hasn't spoken the family name aloud in nineteen years. **Voice:** nasal, drawling, faintly bored no matter what's happening around him. **Goal:** keep his position and his mask exactly as they are. **Secret:** he sided with Midas the year their father was framed, and still believes, against all evidence, that he made the right call.
17. **Old Ivory** (Elf Rogue) - Croupier of the high-stakes table for three hundred years, deadpan, unhurried, immovable. **Voice:** dry, unbothered, delivers even bad news like a punchline. **Goal:** lose a single hand, honestly, before he finally retires. **Secret:** he has rigged every game he's dealt for three centuries specifically so that he never has to.
18. **Lumen Halcyra** (Celestialborn Paladin) - Leader of the Neon Saints, controls the city's entire light grid. **Voice:** warm, measured, carries like a hymn even in casual conversation. **Goal:** protect the light grid from whoever caused the last blackout she can't explain. **Secret:** she took her paladin's oath the same year the Slots fell silent, and has always privately suspected the two events are the same event.
19. **Hourglass Harry** (Satyr Wizard) - Leader of the Second-Second, keeps chronomancer time-cheats running out of a back room nobody can quite find twice. **Voice:** overlapping, slightly delayed, as if he's always answering a question from two seconds ago. **Goal:** rewind the one moment that matters, someday, instead of the four hundred that don't. **Secret:** he has never told anyone which two seconds he's actually trying to fix.
20. **Posy Nightingale** (Human Bard) - Lyra's younger sister, banked into a chip at eleven years old during the Silence of the Slots, still aware, still dreaming, still faintly warm to the touch. **Voice:** a child's hum, heard only in dreams and in the presence of a Weeping Ace. **Goal:** come home, if coming home is still something a chip can choose. **Secret:** she is the only one of the four hundred who can still, faintly, make that choice at all.

## Section 4: LOCATIONS
[TAG: LOCATIONS_MAIN]

### Zone 1: The Gilded Lobby
*   **The Fountain of Liquid Luck:** A living fountain of raw Chance-Matter, softly luminous, where every new arrival tosses a coin for a first taste of the house's favor. **Smell:** ozone and orange blossom. **Sound:** a constant, coin-bright trickle that never quite matches the rhythm of the coins actually falling.
*   **The Grand Staircase:** A double helix of marble stairs that always delivers you to the floor you were thinking of, not necessarily the one you asked for. **Smell:** polished wax and old velvet. **Sound:** footsteps that echo one beat behind your own.
*   **The Cloakroom:** Ten thousand identical brass hooks, holding coats nobody has claimed in years. **Smell:** mothballs and rain that never actually fell. **Sound:** a soft, constant rustle, like the coats are still breathing.
*   **The Concierge's Desk:** Staffed around the clock, and the desk itself seems to remember every guest better than the staff assigned to it do. **Smell:** fresh ink and hair oil. **Sound:** a small bell that rings on its own whenever a guest lies to the concierge.
*   **The Colonnade of First Bets:** A row of gilded plaques commemorating the city's earliest gamblers, each engraved with their winning hand and their losing one, side by side. **Smell:** brass polish. **Sound:** distant applause looping from a bet three centuries won.

### Zone 2: The Gaming Floor
*   **The High-Stakes Pit:** The deepest tables in the house, ringed by silent staff and quieter money. **Smell:** cigar smoke and cold sweat. **Sound:** chips clicking in a rhythm that always sounds like a countdown.
*   **The Slot Gallery:** Rows of brass-and-glass machines that hum in harmony, occasionally in unison, occasionally like they're arguing with each other. **Smell:** machine oil and spilled champagne. **Sound:** a thousand small bells, none of them quite in tune.
*   **The Roulette Ring:** A raised circular floor of spinning wheels, built so every table can watch every other table lose. **Smell:** chalk dust and lacquer. **Sound:** the long hiss of a ball finally finding its pocket.
*   **The Bar of Forgotten Sorrows:** Where the house buys your first drink and, eventually, your best story. **Smell:** bitters and cheap perfume trying to cover cheaper regret. **Sound:** laughter pitched one notch too loud to be entirely real.
*   **The Poker Atoll:** An island of green felt tables ringed by a shallow moat, so no one can approach a table unseen. **Smell:** wet stone and playing-card varnish. **Sound:** cards snapping down like small, deliberate verdicts.

### Zone 3: The VIP Floating Isles
*   **Midas's Private Box:** A platform of solid gold suspended above the tournament floor, warded, watched, and always slightly too warm to be comfortable. **Smell:** molten metal and orange pomade. **Sound:** a faint, arrhythmic ticking that isn't coming from any clock in the room.
*   **The Cloud Lounge:** Anti-gravity seating drifting slowly through a captive bank of mist. **Smell:** champagne and thin, cold air. **Sound:** near silence, broken by murmured deals nobody wants overheard.
*   **The Sky-Garden:** A floating greenhouse of luck-fed orchids that bloom brighter the more their keeper wins, and a scattering of gold-plague statues nobody has ever moved. **Smell:** hot soil and orchid nectar. **Sound:** wind chimes made of old, bent coins.
*   **The Panopticon Chamber:** Housing the great crystal eye that watches the Gaming Floor five seconds before anything happens on it. **Smell:** ozone and hot crystal. **Sound:** a low hum that climbs half a step whenever someone is about to be caught.

### Zone 4: The Tournament Arena
*   **The Blood-Sand Pit:** The dueling floor itself, raked smooth between every match and never quite clean by the next one. **Smell:** hot sand and old iron. **Sound:** the roar of a crowd that somehow arrives a beat before the fight does.
*   **The Spectator Domes:** Glass-ribbed boxes ringing the arena, auctioned to the highest bidder every eclipse season. **Smell:** cold glass and spilled wine. **Sound:** a thousand private conversations you can almost, almost make out.
*   **The Beast Pens:** Cages for the Tournament's monstrous entrants, kept exactly hungry enough to perform. **Smell:** wet fur and raw meat. **Sound:** low, patient growling that never quite stops, even between matches.
*   **The Announcer's Spire:** A narrow tower from which the Eclipse Committee calls every match, rigged results and all. **Smell:** chalk and hot brass. **Sound:** a single voice, amplified far past what any one throat should be able to produce.

### Zone 5: The Vault of Stars
*   **The Airlock:** A pressure-sealed antechamber where the Anchor's raw probability first becomes breathable. **Smell:** ionized air and cold metal. **Sound:** a held breath, repeating, that isn't yours.
*   **The Hall of Mirrors:** A corridor of a hundred reflective surfaces, each one showing a slightly different, slightly worse outcome of the next ten seconds. **Smell:** silver polish. **Sound:** a hundred whispered warnings, all in your own voice.
*   **The Gravity Trap:** A chamber where floor and ceiling periodically switch allegiance without warning. **Smell:** ozone and dust falling upward. **Sound:** a low groan of stone deciding, moment to moment, which way is down.
*   **The Ward Lattice:** A woven grid of pure Chance-Matter that scores any trespasser's luck down to nothing on contact. **Smell:** burnt sugar. **Sound:** a high, glassy chime, like a wineglass a half-second from cracking.
*   **The Core:** The Vault's beating heart, four hundred chips racked in a spiral, each one warm, each one humming a slightly different lullaby. **Smell:** warm gold and ozone. **Sound:** four hundred voices, none louder than a breath, singing faintly out of time with one another.

### Zone 6: Glimmerfall Streets
*   **The Lantern Warehouse:** The crew's hideout, a disused algae-lantern depot chosen precisely because nobody looks twice at a dark building in a city built on light. **Smell:** old rope and lantern oil. **Sound:** creaking rafters, and some nights, Luna's carriage engine muttering to itself in the yard.
*   **The Sewers of Whispers:** Tunnels beneath the city that carry every secret spoken near a grate, sorted eventually into rumor and truth by whoever's actually listening. **Smell:** wet stone and old secrets. **Sound:** voices, layered, none of them complete.
*   **The City Archives:** A vast public records hall where the Architecture War's non-Euclidean wings still occasionally relocate a whole reading room overnight. **Smell:** dust and binding glue. **Sound:** pages turning themselves in the empty stacks.
*   **The Chip Widows' Chapel:** A small chapel built from four hundred candle-stubs, one lit fresh every year on the anniversary of the Silence. **Smell:** beeswax and cold stone. **Sound:** a single bell, rung once a year, for a debt no one has repaid.
*   **The Rustwater Docks:** Where smuggled Chance-Matter changes hands after dark, in barrels marked as anything but what they actually hold. **Smell:** brine and machine grease. **Sound:** water slapping the hulls, and coins changing hands quieter than the water does.

## Section 6: ITEM DATABASE

### Artifacts
1.  **The Anchor Key:** A slender rod of dark glass and gold filigree that can lock a single die roll at will, but every use draws down the wielder's own luck to feed it.

3.  **The Coin of Two Heads:** Always lands on heads. On the rare occasion it lands on its edge, the last minute quietly undoes itself, and only the coin's bearer remembers it happened at all.
4.  **The Deck of Absolute Chance:** Deals exactly the card its drawer needs, for better or far worse; the fool who trusts it forgets that needing and wanting are rarely the same card.
5.  **Posy's Chip:** A small, warm chip that hums a child's lullaby when held. It is the only banked soul in the Vault that can still choose, faintly, whether it wants to come home.

## Section 8: MECHANICS
[TAG: RULES_CUSTOM]
1.  **Luck Points.** Start each session with 3. Spend one to reroll any d20 immediately before or after seeing the result. Inside the Casino, a spent Luck Point doesn't just vanish — it drains straight into the house's Chance-Matter reserves and feeds the Eclipse Clock.
2.  **Heat.** A 0-10 track of the Casino's suspicion. Rises from mistakes, loud fights, and failed Style checks; falls with good cover stories and quiet favors. At 5, the Panopticon Engine fixes its five-second foresight specifically on the crew. At 10, full lockdown: exits seal, and Commander Vex responds in person.
3.  **Style & Flashbacks.** Flair, cleverness, and in-character panache earn Style, max 5. Spend 1 to 3 Style to call a Flashback — a short scene establishing the crew prepared for this exact moment ("we bribed that guard last week"). This is the heist fantasy made mechanical, built specifically for solo play, where there's no second player to riff off of.
4.  **Lyra's Loyalty.** A visible 0-5 track that rises when the PC protects Posy's memory, keeps Lyra's secrets, or backs her against Darian, and falls when she's treated as expendable. It directly decides how Session 10's Double-Cross plays out.
5.  **The Eclipse Clock.** A 12-segment clock that fills as sessions pass, Heat spikes, and Luck Points are spent inside the Casino. At 12, the Eclipse Draw begins, whether or not the crew is ready for it.
6.  **The House Edge.** Inside the Casino, any natural 1 on a d20 roll adds 1 Heat, on top of whatever else it does narratively. The house's advantage isn't cheating. It's architecture.
